Efficacy and Safety of Push-Dose Norepinephrine for
Intraoperative Hypotension Management in Non-Obstetric Surgery:
A Systematic Review
R Suseno Aji, Calcarina Fitriani Retno Wisudarti, Djayanti Sari
Department of Anesthesiology & Intensive Therapy · Faculty of Medicine, Public Health and Nursing
Universitas Gadjah Mada / RSUP Dr. Sardjito, Yogyakarta, Indonesia
◆ Background

Intraoperative hypotension (IOH), defined as MAP <65 mmHg, occurs in up to 30% of non-cardiac surgeries and is independently associated with myocardial injury, acute kidney injury, and increased 30-day mortality. Push-dose (bolus) norepinephrine (NE) has emerged as a practical vasopressor; however, high-quality evidence on its efficacy and safety remains limited.

◆ Objective

To systematically evaluate the efficacy and safety of push-dose NE for IOH in non-obstetric surgery compared to other vasopressors, following PRISMA 2020 guidelines.

◆ Methods

Design: Systematic review · PRISMA 2020

Databases: PubMed, Scopus, ProQuest

PICO: Adults ≥18 yr · Non-obstetric surgery · RCT design

Quality: Cochrane RoB 2 tool

Certainty: GRADE framework

Search string: "Norepinephrine" AND ("push dose" OR "bolus") AND ("intraoperative hypotension" OR "surgery")

◆ Results – Summary

3 RCTs · n = 388 · Push-dose NE 10 mcg/bolus (10 mcg/mL, peripheral IV)

  • vs. Ephedrine: NE superior MAP (98.9 vs 91.5 mmHg; p<0.001), fewer hypotension episodes (p=0.005)
  • vs. Continuous NE (low–mod risk): Comparable AUC MAP <65 (p=0.070), 66% less drug dose (p<0.001)
  • vs. Continuous NE (high risk): Infusion better ARV-MAP stability (p<0.001, d=1.00)
  • Safety: No significant HTN overshoot or arrhythmia; peripheral IV safe — zero extravasation

Hassani (2018)
Iran · RCT · n=56		 Hypertensive, GA Spinal Surgery NE 10 mcg bolus vs Ephedrine 5 mg bolus NE superior MAP (98.9 vs 91.5 mmHg; p<0.001), fewer vasopressor doses, less bradycardia
Thomsen (2026)
Denmark · RCT · n=261		 ASA ≥2, Low–Moderate Risk, Noncardiac NE Bolus 10 mcg/mL vs Continuous Infusion AUC MAP<65 comparable (p=0.070); 66% lower cumulative dose (p<0.001)
Vokuhl (2025)
Germany · RCT · n=71		 ASA II–IV, High-Risk Elective Major Surgery NE Bolus 10 mcg/mL vs Continuous Infusion Continuous infusion better haemodynamic stability (ARV-MAP; p<0.001, d=1.00); peripheral IV safe
◆ Conclusions
Push-dose NE 10 mcg via peripheral IV is effective and safe for IOH in non-obstetric surgery. It is superior to ephedrine and offers comparable efficacy to continuous infusion in low-to-moderate risk patients. For high-risk patients with arterial monitoring, continuous infusion may provide better haemodynamic stability. GRADE Low (⊕⊕○○) — multicenter RCTs with standardised outcome definitions are needed.
