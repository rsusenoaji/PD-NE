Efficacy and Safety of Push-Dose Norepinephrine for Intraoperative
Hypotension Management in Non-Obstetric Surgery: A Systematic Review
R Suseno Aji,  Calcarina Fitriani Retno Wisudarti,  Djayanti Sari
Department of Anesthesiology & Intensive Therapy · Faculty of Medicine, Public Health & Nursing · Universitas Gadjah Mada / RSUP Dr. Sardjito, Yogyakarta, Indonesia
◆ Background

Intraoperative hypotension (IOH), defined as MAP <65 mmHg, occurs in up to 30% of non-cardiac surgeries and is independently linked to myocardial injury, acute kidney injury, and increased 30-day mortality. Push-dose (bolus) norepinephrine (NE) is gaining popularity, but high-quality evidence remains limited.

◆ Objective

To systematically evaluate the efficacy and safety of push-dose norepinephrine for IOH in non-obstetric surgery versus other vasopressors, following PRISMA 2020 guidelines with GRADE certainty assessment.

◆ Methods

Design: Systematic review · PRISMA 2020 compliant

Databases: PubMed · Scopus · ProQuest

Search strategy:
"Norepinephrine" AND ("push dose" OR "bolus") AND
("intraoperative hypotension" OR "surgery")

Inclusion criteria:

  • Adults ≥18 years
  • Non-obstetric elective/emergent surgery
  • RCT design only
  • Push-dose NE as intervention

Quality tools:

  • Risk of Bias: Cochrane RoB 2 tool
  • Certainty of evidence: GRADE framework

Outcome measures: MAP reversal, hypotension duration/AUC, haemodynamic variability (ARV-MAP), safety events (HTN, arrhythmia, extravasation)

◆ Results

3 RCTs · n=388 · NE 10 mcg bolus · Peripheral IV (10 mcg/mL)

  • vs. Ephedrine (Hassani 2018):
    NE superior MAP (98.9 vs 91.5 mmHg; p<0.001), fewer hypotension episodes (p=0.005), less bradycardia
  • vs. Continuous NE · Low–Mod Risk (Thomsen 2026):
    Comparable AUC MAP<65 (p=0.070); 66% lower cumulative dose (p<0.001)
  • vs. Continuous NE · High Risk (Vokuhl 2025):
    Continuous infusion better haemodynamic stability (ARV-MAP; p<0.001, d=1.00)
  • Safety: No significant overshoot HTN or arrhythmia; peripheral IV safe — zero extravasation

Hassani (2018)
Iran · RCT · n=56		 Hypertensive patients, GA spinal surgery NE 10 mcg bolus IV vs Ephedrine 5 mg bolus IV NE superior MAP (98.9 vs 91.5 mmHg; p<0.001); fewer vasopressor doses; less bradycardia
Thomsen (2026)
Denmark · RCT · n=261		 ASA ≥2, low–moderate risk noncardiac surgery NE Bolus 10 mcg/mL vs NE Continuous Infusion AUC MAP<65 comparable (p=0.070); 66% lower cumulative dose (p<0.001)
Vokuhl (2025)
Germany · RCT · n=71		 ASA II–IV, high-risk elective major noncardiac NE Bolus 10 mcg/mL vs NE Continuous Infusion Continuous infusion better haemodynamic stability (ARV-MAP; p<0.001, d=1.00); peripheral IV safe
◆ Conclusions

Push-dose NE 10 mcg via peripheral IV is effective and safe for IOH in non-obstetric surgery.

  • Superior to ephedrine in MAP restoration
  • Comparable to continuous infusion in low–mod risk patients with 66% drug saving
  • Continuous infusion preferred in high-risk patients with arterial monitoring
  • GRADE: Low (⊕⊕○○) — multicenter RCTs needed
