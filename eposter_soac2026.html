Original Research – Systematic Review

Efficacy and Safety of Push-Dose Norepinephrine for Intraoperative Hypotension Management in Non-Obstetric Surgery: A Systematic Review

Rsu Senoaji  |  Department of Anesthesiology, [Institution Name]
Correspondence: [email@institution.ac.id]

Background

Intraoperative hypotension (IOH), defined as mean arterial pressure (MAP) <65 mmHg, occurs in up to 30% of non-cardiac surgeries and is independently associated with myocardial injury, acute kidney injury, and 30-day mortality. Push-dose (bolus) norepinephrine (NE) has emerged as a practical vasopressor for acute IOH management; however, the evidence supporting its efficacy and safety relative to other agents remains limited.

Key Findings
  • vs. Ephedrine: NE 10 mcg bolus significantly superior in maintaining MAP (p<0.001), reducing hypotension episodes (p=0.005) and total vasopressor requirement (p=0.004) in hypertensive patients undergoing spinal surgery.
  • vs. NE Infusion (low–mod risk): No significant difference in AUC hypotension (p=0.070); NE bolus used 66% less total dose vs. infusion (p<0.001).
  • vs. NE Infusion (high-risk): Continuous infusion provided greater BP stability (ARV-MAP, p<0.001, Cohen's d=1.00); AUC hypotension did not differ (p=0.178).
  • Safety: No significant difference in overshoot hypertension; no arrhythmia events documented; peripheral IV administration safe across all studies.
Conclusion
  • Push-dose NE 10 mcg (10 mcg/mL, peripheral IV) is effective and safe for managing IOH in non-obstetric surgery.
  • It is superior to ephedrine in hypertensive patients; comparable to continuous NE infusion for low–moderate risk patients.
  • For high-risk patients with continuous arterial monitoring, continuous infusion may offer better BP stability.
  • Overall evidence certainty is low (GRADE ⊕⊕○○) due to heterogeneity and limited sample size (n=388).
  • High-quality multicenter RCTs with standardised outcomes are needed.
